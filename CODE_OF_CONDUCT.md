# Code of conduct

Concord is a small project run by two people in their spare time. This document
is short because a long one would be a promise about enforcement capacity we do
not have.

## The rule

Be someone the next contributor would want to deal with.

In practice that means: argue about the code, not the person writing it; assume
the patch you are reading was written by someone competent who knew something
you do not; and say what you actually think, early, rather than being agreeable
for three rounds and then rejecting the change. Bluntness is welcome here.
Contempt is not, and the line between them is whether the person on the other
side ends up better informed or just smaller.

Harassment, demeaning comments, and sustained disruption are not on. Neither is
personal or political abuse, unwelcome sexual attention, publishing someone's
private information, or following someone from thread to thread to keep at them.
This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere else the
project is the reason you are talking.

## Scope

Project spaces: this repository and anywhere someone is representing the
project. We are not the internet's conduct police, and we are not going to
adjudicate what you said somewhere unrelated. If behaviour elsewhere makes
someone unsafe *here*, that is in scope.

## Reporting

Open a private report through GitHub's **Report content** on the comment or
account, and — if you are comfortable doing so — open a GitHub issue saying only
that you have done it and that you would rather discuss it privately. Both
maintainers ([MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md)) will see it, and one of us will
find a private channel with you from there.

There is no third party to escalate to. Two maintainers is the whole project, so
a report about one of us goes to the other, and we would rather you sent it than
sat on it.

## What happens

We will read it and respond, in most cases within a week. What follows is
proportionate and boring: usually a private word, sometimes an edited or deleted
comment, occasionally a block. We will tell the person what the problem was
rather than acting silently, and we will not discuss a reporter's identity with
the person reported without asking the reporter first.

We do not run a formal appeals process. If you think we got it wrong, say so and
we will look again.
